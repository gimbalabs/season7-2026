## Gimbalabs Primary Driver

To provide open, replicable safe spaces to learn, to explore, and to empower individuals and organizations anywhere so that we can solve meaningful problems

Note:

Gimbalabs funds experiments, not just activities. Every experimental proposal must produce evidence, reusable knowledge, and, where relevant, a testable path toward future income.

## Proposal Driver

**Current Situation:** Gimbalabs has a look people recognise: the hexagon "g" logo, the teal colour and the hexagon background. But there is no single place to get it. There is no page where you can download the logo, copy the exact colours or check which font to use. So when someone needs to make a Gimbalabs slide, event card or post, they screenshot the website or the GitHub picture and work from that. The name is also written two ways ("Gimbalabs" on most pages, "GimbaLabs" on the Dandelion page). Season 7 starts this fall, and it will bring new people making a lot of new material.

**Effects:** Because everyone starts from screenshots, the logo comes out blurry or slightly wrong, colours are guessed, and each program slowly drifts toward its own look. It takes people longer than it should, and Gimbalabs looks less consistent to partners and newcomers. We have not measured this yet. This proposal measures it.

**Need:** One public page where anyone can download the official Gimbalabs logos, colours and fonts, with simple rules for using them. Big projects already do this: Cardano has a brand assets page (cardano.org/brand-assets) and Spotify has one (newsroom.spotify.com/media-kit/logo-and-brand-assets). Gimbalabs should have one too.

## Proposal

### 1. Title or Brief Description

Gimbalabs Public Brand Kit: the official logos, colours, fonts and simple rules, free to download in one place.

### 2. Background or Rationale

**What is a brand kit:** a brand kit is the "official box" of a brand. It holds the real logo files, the exact colour codes, the fonts, and a few rules (like "don't stretch the logo"). Anyone can open it and make something that looks right the first time.

**How others do it:**
- **Cardano** (cardano.org/brand-assets) shows each logo version with "Download SVG" buttons in white, black and blue, a "download everything" zip, its colour codes, its font, and short rules on minimum size, the space to leave around the logo, what not to do, and how to show it next to a partner's logo.
- **Spotify** (newsroom.spotify.com/media-kit/logo-and-brand-assets) keeps it even simpler: its logo in green, black and white, each one a click to download, plus one "Download All" button.

This proposal builds the same thing for Gimbalabs, using only what Gimbalabs already has. Nothing is redesigned or invented. It uses the official Gimbalabs logo files, the colours already on gimbalabs.com and the hexagon background, and the fonts the website already names.

**Who will do it:** I am Jethro Adebisi, a UI/UX and brand designer. I am the UX Designer at BlockPrint, a Cardano builder community, and I have led a Cardano x Gimbalabs session.

**Why this is an experiment, not just a task:** Harsha Gullapalli's "What Is Gimbalabs?" says "An experiment is useful when it leaves evidence someone else can use." A brand kit is a small, clear test of that. We can check whether people actually use it, and whether it saves them time. If it works, any other community can copy the method.

**The experiment:** a public brand kit will make Gimbalabs material faster to make and more consistent.

References:
- cardano.org/brand-assets and newsroom.spotify.com/media-kit/logo-and-brand-assets (examples of public brand kits)
- gimbalabs.com, and "What Is Gimbalabs?" on gimbalabs.com/blog
- github.com/gimbalabs (including season7-2026)

### 3. Intended Outcome

At the end of one month, Gimbalabs will have:

1. **A public brand kit page.** One web page, like Cardano's, where anyone can:
   - download every Gimbalabs logo (the full logo and the "g" on its own) in SVG and PNG, in teal, white and dark navy
   - download everything at once in a single zip file
   - copy the exact colour codes
   - see which fonts to use, with a free option for people who don't have the paid one
   - read the simple rules: smallest size, space around the logo, what not to do, how to place it next to a partner's logo, and how to spell the name
2. **A short brand guide (PDF, about 12 pages)** for people who want more detail: how Gimbalabs talks, its images, and how each program (Dandelion, Plutus PBL, the Piece of Pie yearbook) can have its own look and still feel like Gimbalabs.
3. **Two ready-to-use templates** in Figma, which anyone can duplicate and edit:
   - an event or Open Spaces card
   - a slide deck
4. **An animated logo:** a short (about 3 second) animation of the Gimbalabs logo for videos, livestreams and Open Spaces recordings, in MP4 and GIF, plus a version with a see-through background that drops onto any video.
5. **A public GitHub repository** (for example `gimbalabs/brand`) holding all the files, with a short note on how anyone can suggest a change.
6. **A short report** saying what was used, whether it saved time, and what to improve.

**Success means:** people use the kit, it makes a standard item (like an event card) much faster to make, and the people who try it say they would use it again (see section 6).

### 4. Details of the Proposal

The work takes four weeks, one step a week.

**Week 1: Gather and agree.**
- Collect everything Gimbalabs already uses: the official logo files, the colours, the fonts, and examples from the website, Dandelion, Plutus PBL and the yearbook.
- Meet the core team once to agree a few things:
  - which logo versions are official
  - the exact colours
  - the correct spelling of the name
  - whether Gimbalabs has a licence for its paid font
  - how the programs should relate to the main brand
- Write every decision down so nobody has to ask again.

**Week 2: Design.**
- Prepare all the logo files (SVG and PNG, every colour version).
- Design the brand guide.
- Share the draft in an Open Spaces session and collect feedback.

**Week 3: Build.**
- Build the public brand kit page with all the downloads and the "download everything" zip.
- Make the two templates.
- Make the animated logo.
- Make the changes from the Week 2 feedback.

**Week 4: Launch and test.**
- Publish the page and the repository, and announce them in Discord and at Open Spaces, with a short video walkthrough.
- Run a simple test: 4 volunteers each make the same event card, once without the kit and once with it, and we time both.
- Ask the people who tried it a few short questions.
- Write and publish the report.

Technical notes:
- The page can live on gimbalabs.com or on a free GitHub page; the core team chooses in Week 1.
- The free font option means anyone can follow the guide without paying for a font. The paid font is never shared.

### 5. Impact and Benefits

**Straight away:**
- Nobody has to screenshot the logo or guess the colours again.
- New builders get one link that answers "what does Gimbalabs look like?"
- Partners, event organisers and press get the right files, ready to use.

**Over time:**
- The kit lives in a public repository, so the community can keep it up to date instead of one person holding the files.
- Each program keeps its own personality inside one recognisable family.
- The method can be copied: any community can build a brand kit from what it already has, and test whether people use it. That fits Gimbalabs' "open, replicable" spaces.

**Possible path toward future income:**
- Other Cardano projects and Catalyst teams need brand kits too.
- During the month, I will offer this service to at least three outside projects and record their answers honestly.
- Any paid work follows section 12 (Licensing and Intellectual Property).

### 6. Evaluation Criteria

| What we check | How | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Time saved | 4 volunteers make the same event card without and with the kit; we time both | With the kit it takes half the time or less |
| Use | Count downloads and items made with the kit in the week after launch, from the repository and Discord | 5 or more items made by 3 or more people |
| Usefulness | A few short questions to the people who tried it | Most say they would use it again |
| Gaps | Count anything people needed that the kit didn't have | Every gap listed in the report, with a plan |

### 10. Potential Risks and Mitigation Strategies

| Risk | What we do about it |
|---|---|
| The core team wants a new look, not the current one | This kit uses the current look only. A redesign, if wanted, can be a separate proposal |
| A logo file is not the latest version | Each logo has one master file, so a newer version replaces it everywhere in one step |
| Nobody knows whether Gimbalabs has a licence for its paid font | The kit offers a free font option, and the paid font is never shared |
| People don't use the kit | It is announced in Discord and at Open Spaces with a short video, and I answer questions in Discord during launch week |
| The kit goes out of date | It lives in a public repository where anyone can suggest a change |

### 11. Review and Adaptation Plan

- **When we review:**
  - end of Week 1 (decisions agreed)
  - end of Week 2 (draft reviewed at Open Spaces)
  - launch
  - the final report
- **What makes us change course:**
  - **The kit doesn't save time.** If the timed test shows no saving, the templates are improved and the report says why.
  - **Something in the kit is wrong.** If the core team corrects anything, it is fixed within a week.
  - **Other projects want one.** If outside projects ask for a brand kit, any paid work is agreed separately, following section 12.

### 12. Licensing and Intellectual Property

**What Gimbalabs owns:** the Gimbalabs name, the logos, the hexagon background image and all program material (Dandelion, Plutus PBL, the Piece of Pie yearbook). The kit only collects and explains these. Gimbalabs keeps full ownership.

Licences for what this project creates:
- **The guide's text, layouts and the templates:** Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 (CC BY-SA 4.0), so other communities can reuse the method and the templates.
- **The web page's code:** MIT License.
- **The Gimbalabs logos inside the kit, including the animated logo:** not covered by the open licences above. They stay Gimbalabs' trademarks, used under Gimbalabs' own terms, the way Cardano points people to its trademark policy.

Existing material to be used:
- **The free font option:** open licence (SIL Open Font License).
- **Gimbalabs' paid font:** named in the guide but never shared.
- **Screenshots and photos:** from Gimbalabs' own public pages, credited in the guide.

**Credit:** I may show the kit in my portfolio, credited as made for Gimbalabs. Any paid brand-kit work for other projects (using the method, not Gimbalabs' logos) is agreed separately, and any revenue share would be decided upon before it starts.
