# The Sustainability Hackathon

**Status:** Draft  
**Working draft for review by:** Harsha, Newman, and the Gimbalabs community

## Gimbalabs Primary Driver

> To provide open, replicable safe spaces to learn, to explore, and to empower individuals and organizations anywhere so that we can solve meaningful problems.

**Note:** Gimbalabs funds experiments, not just activities. Every experimental proposal must produce evidence, reusable knowledge, and—where relevant—a testable path toward future income.

## Proposal Driver

### Current Situation

The Piece of Pie Hackathon demonstrated that builders can learn, collaborate, and deliver working products through shared experimentation and accountability.

However, many projects in the Cardano ecosystem depend on grants or external funding rather than sustainable income from users. Building a product and developing a sustainable business require different skills.

### Effects

When external funding ends, projects risk losing momentum, becoming inactive, or disappearing. This limits the long-term impact of Gimbalabs' efforts and leaves builders without the independence and economic opportunities their products could provide.

### Need

Gimbalabs needs builders and projects to develop the skills, relationships, and sustainable revenue models that enable them to support themselves independently of external funding, while continuing to learn and grow together.

## Proposal

### 1. Title or Brief Description

**The Sustainability Hackathon**

A community-driven hackathon helping builders develop the business skills, relationships, and recurring revenue needed to sustain their projects.

The hackathon will also serve as an experiment to develop a repeatable sustainability program that Gimbalabs could offer to other organizations and sponsors.

### 2. Background or Rationale

The Piece of Pie Hackathon demonstrated that shared experimentation, accountability, and community support help builders learn and deliver working products.

The Sustainability Hackathon builds on this approach, focusing on the next challenge: acquiring customers, generating recurring revenue, and developing financially sustainable projects.

- **For builders:** An opportunity to develop business skills, strengthen social connections, and gain confidence through collaborative learning and experimentation.
- **For Gimbalabs:** An opportunity to test whether the Builder Season hackathon model can be extended into a reusable, potentially commercially viable approach to supporting project sustainability.

### 3. Intended Outcome

#### For Participating Builders and Projects

- Projects establish realistic, measurable sustainability goals based on their operating costs and reasonable compensation for continued development and maintenance.
- Builders develop practical business skills, confidence, and lasting peer relationships.
- Participating projects make measurable progress toward recurring income, with some achieving their agreed sustainability goals for two consecutive months.

#### For Gimbalabs

- Test whether the collaborative hackathon model effectively supports business development and project sustainability.
- Produce a reusable Sustainability Hackathon playbook, operational tools, templates, and documented lessons learned.
- Evaluate the potential to offer the program as a repeatable, funded service to other organizations and sponsors.
- Strengthen Gimbalabs' value to builders, sponsors, and other ecosystem stakeholders.

### 4. Details of the Proposal

Conduct a Sustainability Hackathon using the Gimbalabs Builder Season model, combining goal setting, accountability, community interaction, business development experiments, and shared learning.

#### Participation

The hackathon will be open to anyone interested in building sustainable products, with two participation tracks.

##### Builder Track

- Teams still developing their products may participate in the broader builder community.
- These teams are not eligible for rewards until they meet the requirements of the Sustainability Track.

##### Sustainability Track

- Teams with working products complete an administrator interview to establish realistic, measurable sustainability goals.
- Teams gain access to dedicated discussions and become eligible to qualify for Sustainability Track rewards.
- Teams may change members or pivot their products during the hackathon, subject to review of their agreed sustainability goals.

Enrollment will remain open on a rolling basis during the initial operating period, subject to available resources.

#### Sustainability Goals

Each Sustainability Track team meets with an administrator to jointly establish a realistic, measurable goal.

Goals are project-specific and informed by:

- Operating and maintenance costs
- Reasonable compensation for builders
- Existing revenue and customer activity
- The project's business model and circumstances

Success criteria and verification requirements will be documented during onboarding.

#### Activities and Community Accountability

- Mandatory, biweekly (every other week) live check-ins provide opportunities to share progress, challenges, experiments, and next steps.
- Dedicated community discussions support collaboration and exchange of business strategies.
- Participants practice customer acquisition, marketing, distribution, revenue development, and business operations through peer-led experimentation.
- Teams share progress and lessons learned to contribute to the collective knowledge of the hackathon.
- Sustainability Track teams will be expected to document their experiments and results through monthly blogs, with corresponding social media posts to increase visibility.

#### Sustainability Pie Rewards

- A team qualifies for a **2,000 ADA** Sustainability Track reward after demonstrating achievement of its agreed sustainability goal for two consecutive months.
- Administrators review verifiable evidence against the team's documented success criteria.
- Rewards are distributed in order of verified qualification, while funding remains available.
- The initial reward pool supports **five** qualifying projects.
- Teams that receive a reward exit the reward program but may remain involved in the community.
- Additional sponsorships may expand the number of available rewards.

#### Evaluation Instruments

- During onboarding, participants complete a short baseline survey measuring business confidence, social connections with other builders, and expectations for the hackathon.
- An exit survey will measure changes in these areas, gather lessons learned, and assess whether participants believe the model is valuable and worth repeating.
- Feedback will also be collected from administrators, sponsors, Gimbalabs stakeholders, and other relevant community members.

#### Documentation and Reusable Program Development

Administrators will maintain a GitHub repository and develop reusable materials, including:

- A Sustainability Hackathon playbook
- Onboarding and goal-setting interview templates
- Evaluation surveys and tracking tools
- Sustainability verification procedures
- Lessons learned, examples, and operational guidance

These materials will support future open-source Gimbalabs implementations and potential administration or consulting services for other organizations.

#### Sponsorship

Administrators will seek additional sponsorships to expand the number of Sustainability Track rewards and, where appropriate, increase administration resources.

### 5. Impact and Benefits

#### For Gimbalabs

- Strengthens and expands the Gimbalabs community through collaboration and shared learning.
- Demonstrates Gimbalabs' mission by helping builders develop sustainable projects.
- Develops a repeatable sustainability program, playbook, and operational infrastructure.
- Creates opportunities to offer hackathon administration, facilitation, or consulting services to other organizations.
- Increases visibility through public progress updates, shared experiences, and social media engagement.
- Builds trust through commitments, accountability, and demonstrated results.
- Demonstrates Gimbalabs' governance through community-driven proposals, decision-making, and resource allocation.
- Advances Gimbalabs' culture of decentralized, collaborative, and friendly competitive innovation.

#### For Participants

- Provides a supportive environment with clear goals, accountability, and motivation.
- Develops practical business, marketing, and customer acquisition skills.
- Strengthens relationships, confidence, and opportunities for collaboration.
- Helps projects generate recurring income and reduce dependence on external funding.
- Encourages learning from both successful and unsuccessful experiments.

#### For the Broader Ecosystem

- Increases the potential for useful products and services to become financially sustainable.
- Helps builders develop viable businesses and organizations.
- Supports the transition from prototypes to practical, lasting applications.
- Creates reusable knowledge that other builders and communities can learn from.

### 6. Evaluation Criteria

The Sustainability Hackathon will be evaluated using both quantitative and qualitative measures to assess participant outcomes, community impact, resource effectiveness, and the potential to repeat the program.

#### Quantitative Measures

- Number of participating projects, including enrollments, completions, and dropouts
- Number of projects achieving two consecutive months of their agreed sustainability goals
- Number of projects establishing recurring income without achieving full sustainability
- Number of sponsors secured and total sponsorship funding received
- Number of check-in meetings, attendance levels, and accountability updates
- Number of blogs, public progress updates, and social media posts
- Administrative time, expenses, and other resources used

#### Qualitative Measures

- Participant feedback on motivation, confidence, business skills, and community connections
- Feedback from sponsors, administrators, Gimbalabs stakeholders, and the broader community
- Quality of collaboration, shared experiments, and documented lessons learned
- Overall community sentiment and engagement
- Perceived value of the program and interest in future participation or sponsorship

#### Measuring Change and Learning

- Baseline and exit surveys will measure changes in participants' confidence, business capabilities, social connections, and perceptions of the hackathon's value.
- The number of projects achieving sustainability goals is an important outcome, but not the sole measure of success.
- The hackathon will also be evaluated on its ability to develop participants' capabilities, strengthen the community, generate useful knowledge, and demonstrate a model worth repeating.

#### Gimbalabs Program Outcomes

- Completion of a reusable Sustainability Hackathon playbook
- Development of operational templates, tools, and documented procedures
- Evidence of what works, what does not, and the cost of administering the program
- Identification of potential sponsors or organizations interested in future implementations
- Assessment of the program's feasibility as a repeatable, funded service
- A final retrospective will summarize the results and recommend improvements

### 7. Timeline

The Sustainability Hackathon will operate with rolling enrollment during an initial **eight-month** period. Teams may join at any time during this period, subject to available resources.

The program is intended to remain flexible rather than follow a fixed cohort schedule.

#### Month 1 — Preparation and Launch

- Establish the website, GitHub repository, communication channels, and administrative processes.
- Prepare onboarding interviews, evaluation instruments, and project tracking tools.
- Finalize program rules, eligibility, and verification requirements.
- Begin outreach to participants and sponsors.

#### Months 2–8 — Ongoing Hackathon Activities

- Accept new participants on a rolling basis.
- Conduct onboarding interviews and establish sustainability goals.
- Facilitate biweekly check-ins, business development experiments, and shared learning.
- Collect participant feedback and document lessons learned.
- Verify two consecutive months of sustainability and distribute rewards as teams qualify.

#### Month 8 — Formal Evaluation

- Review participation, project progress, sponsorships, and resource use.
- Evaluate the program's effectiveness and repeatability.
- Assess whether to continue operations, accept additional enrollments, or conclude the hackathon.
- Review the remaining reward funding and commitments to participating teams.

#### Program Closure

A final backstop date will prevent the program from remaining indefinitely inactive while holding unused funds.

- **Gimbalabs community members:** Contribute expertise, collaboration, mentorship, and support.
- **Participating teams:** Pursue their sustainability goals, share progress and lessons learned, and provide evidence of their achievements.

#### Tools and Infrastructure

Existing or low-cost tools will support:

- Website and registration
- GitHub repository and reusable documentation
- Community communication and check-ins
- Evaluation surveys and progress tracking
- Financial and reward administration

### 9. Responsibilities

#### Harsha and Newman — Hackathon Administrators

Harsha and Newman will share responsibility for planning, organizing, and operating the Sustainability Hackathon, including:

- **Setup and coordination:** Establish and maintain the website, repository, registration process, communication channels, and program guidelines.
- **Participant support:** Conduct interviews, agree on sustainability goals, answer questions, and support participating teams.
- **Community engagement:** Monitor communication channels, facilitate check-ins, and encourage collaboration.
- **Communications and sponsorships:** Coordinate public updates, social media activity, and outreach to potential sponsors.
- **Tracking and evaluation:** Monitor progress, collect feedback, verify sustainability achievements, and document lessons learned.
- **Financial administration:** Manage reward allocations and coordinate payouts.
- **Program development:** Create and improve the reusable Gimbalabs Sustainability Hackathon playbook.

Specific responsibilities and time commitments will be agreed upon during the planning phase.

#### Participating Teams

Teams are responsible for:

- Pursuing their agreed sustainability goals
- Participating in accountability and learning activities (blogs, X posts, and check-in meetings)
- Sharing experiments, progress, and lessons learned according to program guidelines
- Providing verifiable evidence when requesting Sustainability Track rewards

### 10. Potential Risks and Mitigation Strategies

| Potential Risk | Mitigation Strategy |
| --- | --- |
| Low participation | Promote the hackathon through Gimbalabs networks and partners. Use an initial interest form to assess demand. |
| Limited sponsorship | Begin with treasury funding, approach multiple sponsors, and operate within available resources. |
| Projects struggle to generate revenue | Encourage experimentation, collaboration, and sharing lessons from unsuccessful approaches. |
| Unrealistic sustainability goals | Review and negotiate goals during onboarding, with documented success criteria. |
| Difficulty verifying sustainability | Agree on evidence requirements in advance and establish a consistent review process. |
| Misunderstandings about rules or rewards | Publish clear guidelines and discuss expectations during onboarding. |
| Participant disengagement or dropout | Maintain regular check-ins, encourage peer support, and allow flexibility as projects evolve. |
| Administrative workload exceeds expectations | Use simple tools, share responsibilities, and review resource use regularly. |
| Program is difficult to replicate | Document procedures, costs, challenges, and lessons throughout the experiment. |
| Program becomes inactive while holding unused funds | Conduct an eight-month evaluation, establish a final closure date, and return unused funds to their original sponsoring organizations according to funding agreements. |

### 11. Review and Adaptation Plan

#### Operational Reviews

Administrators will review participation, project progress, and emerging challenges through biweekly check-ins and ongoing communication with teams.

#### Gimbalabs Governance Reviews

Progress will be reported during monthly Gimbalabs Open Governance project reviews, including participation, sponsorships, community engagement, resource use, and significant challenges.

#### Continuous Learning

A running Lessons Learned section will document observations, participant feedback, experiments, challenges, and opportunities for improvement.

These findings will contribute to the Sustainability Hackathon playbook and support improvements during the program.

#### Formal Evaluation

- At eight months, administrators will evaluate the program's effectiveness, sustainability, and potential for continuation or replication.
- The review will inform decisions about further enrollment, additional sponsorships, extension, or closure.
- A final retrospective will document overall results and recommendations for future implementations.

### 12. Licensing and Intellectual Property

#### Participant Intellectual Property

- Participating teams retain ownership and control of their products, software, business models, and other intellectual property.
- Each team determines its own licensing, revenue-sharing, and commercial arrangements.
- Participation in the hackathon does not transfer ownership of participant intellectual property to Gimbalabs or other participants.
- Confidential business information and project-specific materials will not be publicly disclosed without appropriate permission.

#### Hackathon Materials and Model

- The Sustainability Hackathon is intended to produce reusable documentation, educational resources, templates, operational processes, and a program playbook.
- The intention is to make these materials openly available with appropriate attribution while preserving Gimbalabs' ability to reuse, adapt, and commercialize its experience and services.
- Gimbalabs may offer paid administration, facilitation, training, or consulting services based on the hackathon model.

#### Existing Intellectual Property

- Existing intellectual property contributed by participants or other parties remains subject to its original ownership, licensing, and permissions.
- Any reuse of third-party materials will respect applicable license terms.

#### Privacy and Consent

- Participants will be informed about what information is collected for evaluation and sustainability verification.
- Public use of identifiable participant information, confidential business details, or project-specific results will require appropriate consent.
