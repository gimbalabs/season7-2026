# Sustainability Hackathon

Working draft for review by Harsha, Newman, and the Gimbalabs community.

## Proposal Driver

### Current Situation

The Piece of Pie Hackathon demonstrated that builders can learn, collaborate, and deliver working products through shared experimentation and accountability.

However, many projects in the Cardano ecosystem rely on grants or other outside funding. Building a product and building a sustainable business require different skills.

### Effects

When external funding ends, projects risk losing momentum, becoming inactive, or disappearing. This limits the long-term impact of Gimbalabs' efforts and leaves builders without the independence and economic opportunities their products could provide.

### Needs

Gimbalabs needs a way to help builders develop business skills, customer relationships, and sources of income that can sustain their work while they continue learning together.

## Proposal

### 1. Title or Brief Description

The Sustainability Hackathon

A community-driven hackathon helping people turn products, services, and skills into sustainable work through real-world experiments and shared learning.

The hackathon will also serve as an experiment to develop a repeatable sustainability program that Gimbalabs could offer to other organizations and sponsors.

### 2. Background or Rationale

The Piece of Pie Hackathon demonstrated that shared experimentation, accountability, and community support help builders learn and deliver working products.

The Sustainability Hackathon creates the same space to focus, but on the next challenge: talking with customers, testing whether an offering solves a problem, learning from feedback, and finding repeatable support or income.

For participants: A chance to learn business skills, test ideas with customers, build confidence, and work alongside peers.

For Gimbalabs: An opportunity to test whether the Builder Season hackathon model can be extended into a reusable, potentially commercially viable approach to supporting project sustainability.

### 3. Intended Outcome

#### For Participating Builders and Projects

- Participants agree on realistic, measurable sustainability goals that reflect the costs, income needs, time available, and personal circumstances of the people doing the work.
- Builders develop practical business skills, confidence, and lasting peer relationships.
- Participants test customer demand and make progress toward repeatable income or support. Some demonstrate their agreed sustainability goals for two consecutive months.

#### For Gimbalabs

- Test whether the collaborative hackathon model effectively supports business development and project sustainability.
- Produce a reusable Sustainability Hackathon playbook, operational tools, templates, and documented lessons learned.
- Evaluate the potential to offer the program as a repeatable, funded service to other organizations and sponsors.
- Strengthen Gimbalabs' value to builders, sponsors, and other ecosystem stakeholders.

### 4. Details of the Proposal

Run a Sustainability Hackathon using the Gimbalabs Builder Season approach: personal goals, practical experiments, shared learning, and clear evidence of results.

#### Participation

The hackathon welcomes builders, freelancers, and teams with an existing product, service, or marketable skill that they are ready to test with real customers. A working software product is not required.

#### Enrollment and Eligibility

Participants may join alone or as a team. To qualify for rewards, they must have something real to offer and an accountable human who can explain the work and its sustainability goal. AI agents may assist, but a human remains responsible.

The program will launch with at least 10 qualified teams. A cohort of 20–30 is considered a successful level of participation, with a maximum of 30 active teams at one time.

Teams may change members, revise their services, or pivot their products. Changes that affect agreed goals or evidence must be reviewed with an administrator.

Enrollment will be open during Months 1–3, subject to capacity and reward availability, and will close at the end of Month 3. Teams beyond the limit may join a waitlist during this window. No new teams, including waitlisted teams, will be admitted after enrollment closes.

#### Individual Onboarding and Sustainability Goals

Before onboarding, each team completes a short structured questionnaire. Then it meets individually with an administrator for a recorded goal-setting conversation lasting no more than 30 minutes.

During onboarding, the team and administrator will confirm the accountable human, agree on a meaningful sustainability goal, and decide what evidence will show it has been met. Goals may consider:

- Operating and maintenance costs.
- Income or compensation the participant wants or needs.
- Available time and other personal limits.
- Existing customers, revenue, support, and the business model.

The team and administrator will record the agreed goal, the two-month qualification period, and the evidence required. Participants will confirm the written agreement. Recordings will be kept private under the program’s data-handling rules.

#### Activities, Learning, and Accountability

Every other week, Gimbalabs will host a group Open Spaces-style session where teams can share progress, questions, and lessons. Attendance is encouraged but not required.

A shared community channel will support questions, peer help, and exchange of business ideas between sessions.

Participants are encouraged to get out from behind their screens: meet customers, test value, ask for payment or support, and improve their offering based on feedback.

Optional emails or newsletters will share timely business questions and resources. This is not a required course or fixed curriculum.

Each participating team must submit one short progress blog post each month describing what it tried, what it learned, and its next steps. Public publication and related social posts are encouraged; the required sharing channel and privacy rules must be finalized before launch.

#### Sustainability Pie Rewards

A team becomes eligible for a 2,000 ADA Sustainability Pie reward after meeting its agreed sustainability goal for two consecutive months and providing the agreed evidence.

Administrators will assess evidence against the goal recorded at onboarding, using the same standard of verification for every team even when goals differ.

Rewards are distributed in order of verified qualification while money remains in the reward pool. Enrollment does not guarantee a reward.

The initial 10,000 ADA reward pool supports five qualifying teams, even if 20–30 teams participate.

After receiving a reward, a team leaves the reward program but may continue joining community activities.

Sponsors may fund more rewards; any changes to amounts or qualification rules must be announced clearly.

Before rewards are paid, Gimbalabs will confirm the appropriate tax and reporting process for prizes, including payments to people outside the United States. Prize payments must not automatically be treated as contractor work.

#### Evaluation Instruments

The onboarding form will include a short baseline survey about business confidence, existing relationships, and expectations.

An exit survey will measure changes in these areas, gather lessons learned, and assess whether participants believe the model is valuable and worth repeating.

Feedback will also be collected from administrators, sponsors, Gimbalabs stakeholders, and other relevant community members.

#### Documentation and Reusable Program Development

Administrators will keep a GitHub repository and maintain reusable materials, including:

- A Sustainability Hackathon playbook.
- Registration questions, goal-setting forms, and a short onboarding meeting guide.
- Evaluation surveys and tracking tools.
- Evidence and reward-verification procedures, including basic identity and anti-abuse checks.
- Lessons learned, examples, and operational guidance.

These resources will be openly documented where practical so Gimbalabs can improve and repeat the program or offer it to partners.

#### Sponsorship

Administrators will seek sponsors to fund more Sustainability Pie awards and, if needed, additional administration.

### 6. Evaluation Criteria

The Sustainability Hackathon will be evaluated using both quantitative and qualitative measures to assess participant outcomes, community impact, resource effectiveness, and the potential to repeat the program.

#### Quantitative Measures

- Number of qualified teams enrolled, active teams, completions, and dropouts; compare results with the 10-team minimum and 20–30-team target.
- Number of projects achieving two consecutive months of their agreed sustainability goals.
- Number of teams that gain customers or recurring income even if they do not qualify for rewards.
- Number of sponsors secured and total sponsorship funding received.
- Number of group Open Spaces held, attendance, and community participation (attendance is optional).
- Number of required monthly progress posts received and optional public posts.
- Administrative hours, costs, onboarding meetings, and time needed to verify goals and awards.

#### Qualitative Measures

- Participant feedback on motivation, confidence, business skills, and community connections.
- Feedback from sponsors, administrators, Gimbalabs stakeholders, and the broader community.
- Quality of collaboration, shared experiments, and documented lessons learned.
- Overall community sentiment and engagement.
- Perceived value of the program and interest in future participation or sponsorship.

#### Measuring Change and Learning

Baseline and exit surveys will measure changes in participants' confidence, business capabilities, social connections, and perceptions of the hackathon's value.

The number of projects achieving sustainability goals is an important outcome, but not the sole measure of success.

The hackathon will also be evaluated on its ability to develop participants' capabilities, strengthen the community, generate useful knowledge, and demonstrate a model worth repeating.

#### Gimbalabs Program Outcomes

- Completion of a reusable Sustainability Hackathon playbook.
- Development of operational templates, tools, and documented procedures.
- Evidence of what works, what does not, and the cost of administering the program.
- Identification of potential sponsors or organizations interested in future implementations.
- Assessment of the program's feasibility as a repeatable, funded service.

A final retrospective will summarize the results and recommend improvements.

### 7. Timeline

The hackathon will run for six months, with preparation and launch during Month 1, enrollment open during Months 1–3, continued activities during Months 4–5, and formal evaluation and closure in Month 6. Teams need two consecutive months of verified sustainability before a reward can be paid.

Enrollment and the two-month qualification windows may overlap during the first three months. Enrollment closes at the end of Month 3; the remaining three months support enrolled teams, reward verification, evaluation, and closure. No more than 30 teams will be active at a time.

#### Month 1 — Preparation and Launch

- Set up registration, the website, GitHub repository, meeting space, and community channel.
- Prepare the onboarding questionnaire, recording and consent process, goal agreement, surveys, and tracking tools.
- Confirm eligibility, enrollment limits, evidence standards, prize funding, tax handling, and privacy rules before taking registrations.
- Invite teams and approach potential sponsors. Launch after at least 10 qualified teams have enrolled.

#### Months 2–3 — Enrollment and Activities

- Enroll qualified teams while spaces remain available; use a waitlist once 30 active teams are reached. Close enrollment, including admissions from the waitlist, at the end of Month 3.
- Hold individual onboarding meetings of no more than 30 minutes; document and confirm each team’s goals.
- Host optional biweekly group Open Spaces and collect required monthly blog posts.
- Offer optional learning resources, collect feedback, and improve the playbook as the program runs.
- Verify teams that complete two consecutive months of their agreed goals and award available prizes.

#### Months 4–5 — Activities and Qualification

- Continue supporting enrolled teams; enrollment remains closed.
- Host optional biweekly group Open Spaces and collect required monthly blog posts.
- Support teams in completing two consecutive months of their agreed sustainability goals.
- Verify evidence, award available prizes, and continue gathering feedback and improving the playbook.

#### Month 6 — Formal Evaluation and Closure

- Review enrollment, completions, dropouts, prize claims, sponsorship, and administrator workload.
- Evaluate what participants learned and whether Gimbalabs has developed a useful, repeatable program.
- Complete outstanding reward reviews and close the six-month program, taking existing participant commitments into account.
- Recommend improvements and assess interest in a future program.
- Report remaining reward funds and how they will be handled under sponsor or treasury agreements.

#### Program Closure

The program will close at the end of Month 6. Confirm and publish the exact calendar dates for enrollment closure, final reward claims, and program closure before launch so all enrolled teams understand the time available to qualify.

### 10. Potential Risks and Mitigation Strategies

| Potential Risk | Mitigation Strategy |
| --- | --- |
| Low participation | Recruit through Gimbalabs and partners, assess early interest, and launch only after 10 qualified teams enroll. |
| Limited sponsorship | Begin with treasury funding, approach multiple sponsors, and operate within available resources. |
| Projects struggle to generate revenue | Encourage experimentation, collaboration, and sharing lessons from unsuccessful approaches. |
| Unrealistic sustainability goals | Review and negotiate goals during onboarding, with documented success criteria. |
| Difficulty verifying sustainability | Agree on evidence requirements in advance and establish a consistent review process. |
| Misunderstandings about rules or rewards | Publish clear guidelines and discuss expectations during onboarding. |
| Participant disengagement or dropout | Use optional group Open Spaces, monthly written updates, and a clear process for leaving or changing goals. |
| Administrative workload exceeds expectations | Use simple tools, share responsibilities, and review resource use regularly. |
| Program is difficult to replicate | Document procedures, costs, challenges, and lessons throughout the experiment. |
| Program becomes inactive while holding unused funds | Conduct a six-month evaluation, close the program at the end of Month 6, and return unused funds to their original sponsoring organizations according to funding agreements. |
| Fraudulent or agent-only entries | Confirm the accountable human and actual product or service during onboarding; keep proportionate evidence and eligibility checks. |
| Different goals feel unfair | Agree on team-specific goals but use consistent standards of evidence and an appeal or review process. |
| More qualified teams than funded awards | Publish the five-award limit and first-verified rule before enrollment; seek sponsors but do not promise extra prizes. |
| Recording and privacy concerns | Ask for informed consent; limit access to recordings and private business evidence; set a retention period. |
| Incorrect prize tax treatment | Confirm domestic and international prize reporting and recipient information before payments. |
| Monthly posts become burdensome | Use a short, repeatable prompt; require substantive updates but not polished essays. |

### 12. Licensing and Intellectual Property

#### Participant Intellectual Property

- Participating teams retain ownership and control of their products, software, business models, and other intellectual property.
- Each team determines its own licensing, revenue-sharing, and commercial arrangements.
- Participation in the hackathon does not transfer ownership of participant intellectual property to Gimbalabs or other participants.
- Confidential business information and project-specific materials will not be publicly disclosed without appropriate permission.

#### Hackathon Materials and Model

- The Sustainability Hackathon is intended to produce reusable documentation, educational resources, templates, operational processes, and a program playbook.
- The intention is to make these materials openly available with appropriate attribution while preserving Gimbalabs' ability to reuse, adapt, and commercialize its experience and services.
- Gimbalabs may offer paid administration, facilitation, training, or consulting services based on the hackathon model.

#### Existing Intellectual Property

- Existing intellectual property contributed by participants or other parties remains subject to its original ownership, licensing, and permissions.
- Any reuse of third-party materials will respect applicable license terms.

#### Privacy and Consent

Participants will be told what is recorded and collected during enrollment, goal-setting, surveys, and prize verification.

Public use of names, recordings, business details, or project results will require appropriate consent. Teams should be able to share the required monthly update without revealing confidential information.
